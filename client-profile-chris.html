Read this first

Silence is not disinterest, and brevity is not displeasure

Chris went several weeks without responding to friendly check-ins, then signed a management agreement within about twenty-four hours of deciding to move. Both of those are normal for him and neither is a signal about how the relationship is going.

He does not reply to text messages. He reads them and responds by email. Any read of his responsiveness based on a text thread will be wrong — the gaps are a channel mismatch, not avoidance.

When he does engage, he engages fast and expects us to match. His two-word reply asking for a call — "Now. If you can." — is the clearest thing in his file. A decisive client who is ready and finds us unavailable is a client we have made wait, and that is expensive with this style.

Do
  • Send everything substantive by email
  • Lead with the ask and the date — put it in the first line
  • Attach a reason the deadline matters, not pressure
  • Keep messages short; match his register
  • Be ready to act the moment he engages
  • Confirm completion in one line and move on
Don't
  • Rely on text for anything that needs a reply
  • Open with warm preamble before the ask
  • Read short replies as annoyance and over-compensate
  • Chase with repeated friendly check-ins — they don't work
  • Assume silence means he's gone
  • Leave an open-ended request with no date attached
What the record shows

Why we're reading him this way

He moves on his own clock, then expects immediacy

"Now." / "If you can."

No greeting, no preamble, no softening. When he decided he was ready for a call, he wanted it that moment. That is the defining behavior in his file.

He honors deadlines once they carry a specific time

He said he would return the signed agreement "in the morning" and did not. After one direct nudge with a stated reason — we cannot collect the key without signed authority — he replied "on the way to the office and I'll turn it around before 10" and delivered. Vague commitments slip; specific ones hold.

Warmth did not move him — clarity did

Four courteous follow-ups over several weeks produced nothing. What produced action was a short, no-pressure note that his file was being closed. He does not respond to being pursued; he responds when something is about to be settled without him.

The practical version

This is not a tactic to repeat for its own sake. The lesson is simply that with Chris, a clear statement of what happens next outperforms a friendly reminder — every time.

He gives no relational signal at all

No pleasantries returned, no emoji, nothing personal volunteered beyond logistics. This is not coldness and it should not be corrected for. Responding to terseness with longer, warmer messages reads to a D as wasting their time.

By function

How this applies to your role

Onboarding

Email, with a date and a dependency attached

Send onboarding items by email with the deadline and the reason it matters in the first two lines — "we need X by Thursday or photography moves to the following week." Avoid multi-step packets with no due dates; they will sit. If something is time-critical, a text saying "sent you an email, needs a reply today" is the right use of text.

Maintenance & Client Experience

Recommendation, cost, deadline — in that order

Give him the bottom line first, then the detail underneath for reference. He will decide quickly and will not want a discussion. Don't bury the number or the decision date below background.

Leasing

Give the recommendation, not the tour of the data

Lead with the number and the expected time to lease. The analysis goes underneath as support — he may well read it, unlike some owners, but he wants the conclusion first and the evidence second.

Everyone

Never wait on him without a fallback

Anything that depends on his input needs a hard date tied to a real consequence, or a plan that works without him. He is not unreliable — he is intermittent, and the schedule has to absorb that rather than break on it.

Watch items

Two things to keep in view

⚑ He has gone quiet once already, mid-process

He proposed the August 1 turnover himself, then went silent for several weeks while clearing furniture. Expect the same pattern during onboarding — not as a warning sign, but as a planning assumption. Build dates that don't collapse if he takes ten days to answer.

Sample size

This profile rests on a small number of short messages. The channel preference and the deadline behavior are well evidenced; the rest is directional. Adjust it as the team gets real working time with him.
